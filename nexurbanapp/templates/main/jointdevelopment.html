FAQ

Joint Development Questions Answered

01

What is a joint development in real estate?

A joint development is a partnership where a landowner contributes land and a developer contributes construction expertise and capital, with both parties sharing in the resulting revenue or completed units, rather than the landowner simply selling the land outright.

02

Why would a landowner choose a joint development instead of selling the land?

A joint development can generate significantly higher long-term value than a one-time land sale, since the landowner shares in the appreciation and revenue of the finished project rather than accepting land-only pricing.

03

How is revenue typically split in a Dubai joint development?

Revenue-sharing models vary — common structures include a percentage of unit sales, a fixed number of completed units to the landowner, or a hybrid of upfront payment plus profit share. The right model depends on land value, project scale and market conditions.

04

What due diligence is done before starting a joint development?

Nexurban assesses zoning and permitted use, market demand and absorption rates, developer track record, and development economics before recommending any partnership, to confirm the project is commercially viable before terms are finalised.

05

How long does a typical joint development project take in Dubai?

Timelines vary by project scale and approvals, but most joint developments — from structuring to completed handover — span roughly 2 to 4 years, depending on plot size, design complexity and construction phasing.
