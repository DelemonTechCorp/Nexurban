FAQ

Joint Venture Questions.

A real estate joint venture is a partnership where two or more parties — typically a landowner, investor, and/or developer — combine resources such as land, capital and expertise to develop a property project and share in the resulting profits and risks.

Common participants include landowners without development capacity, investors seeking direct project-level returns, and developers seeking access to land or additional capital. Nexurban structures these relationships so each party's contribution and return are clearly defined.

Profit-sharing structures vary widely — from fixed land value plus percentage upside, to full equity partnerships with proportional returns. The right structure depends on each party's contribution, risk tolerance and capital commitment.

JVs in Dubai are commonly structured through a Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV), a shareholder agreement, or a development management agreement, depending on the scale and complexity of the project. Nexurban coordinates with legal advisors to determine the appropriate structure for each deal.

We evaluate potential partners against the specific project's requirements — assessing financial capacity, development track record, strategic alignment and risk appetite — before facilitating any introduction, to ensure long-term partnership viability.
